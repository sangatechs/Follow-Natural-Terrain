How Game World Generation Creates Rivers That Follow Natural Terrain

With pkr 98, readers can explore gaming concepts such as procedural river generation and learn why following elevation and drainage paths helps rivers fit more naturally into a generated landscape. A river that ignores terrain — flowing uphill or cutting across ridges at random angles — immediately reads as artificial. Rivers that follow genuine elevation gradients feel like they belong to the world they inhabit.

Real rivers flow downhill because water moves from high to low elevation under gravity. Procedural river systems mimic this by tracing paths that follow the terrain's descent. Starting from a high-elevation source, the river follows a path that consistently moves to lower adjacent terrain points until it reaches a body of water, a map edge, or a sufficiently low depression.

River Generation Stages

1

Source Selection

Rivers begin at elevated locations — mountain slopes, highland plateaus, or other high-terrain positions. The source must be high enough above the destination to provide a meaningful downhill path.

2

Flow Direction

At each step, the river moves to the adjacent terrain point with the lowest elevation. This steepest-descent approach mimics how water naturally flows downhill following the terrain gradient.

3

Drainage Path

As the river descends, it carves a drainage network. Tributary streams may join the main channel from smaller side valleys, increasing the river's width downstream as it collects flow from a larger area.

4

Destination

Rivers terminate at seas, lakes, or map edges. The endpoint is determined by the terrain — where the downhill path naturally arrives determines where the river meets its destination.

  • River sources are placed at elevated terrain positions with a clear downhill path below them
  • Flow direction follows the steepest descent to adjacent lower terrain at each step
  • Tributaries form naturally where multiple downhill paths converge toward the same channel
  • Rivers terminate at the lowest accessible point in their drainage area
